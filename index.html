Past Tenses

Four ways to talk about the past: finished events, actions in progress, earlier past events and actions continuing up to a past moment.

QUICK GUIDE

Choose the right tense

Past SimpleWhat happened? A finished action or event.
Past ContinuousWhat was happening at that moment?
Past PerfectWhat had happened before another past event?
Past Perfect ContinuousHow long had it been happening before a past moment?
StorytellingPast Simple moves the story forward; Past Continuous sets the scene.
Timeline cluePast Perfect forms connect two past moments.

Remember: Signal words can help, but meaning and timeline decide the tense.

Mini-rules

1. Past Simple

Use: completed actions, past habits and a sequence of events.

Positive: subject + V2/-ed · Negative: didn't + V1 · Question: Did + subject + V1?

We visited the museum yesterday. · She didn't see the message. · Did they arrive on time?

Common markers: yesterdaylast weekin 2022two days ago

2. Past Continuous

Use: an action in progress at a specific past time; background action; an action interrupted by another event.

Positive: was/were + V-ing · Negative: wasn't/weren't + V-ing · Question: Was/Were + subject + V-ing?

At 8 p.m. I was studying. · We were walking when it started to rain.

Common markers: at that momentwhilewhenall evening

3. Past Perfect

Use: an action completed before another past action or past time.

Positive: had + V3 · Negative: hadn't + V3 · Question: Had + subject + V3?

By the time we arrived, the film had started. · She was upset because she had lost her keys.

Common markers: by the timealreadybeforeafternever

4. Past Perfect Continuous

Use: an activity that continued for a period before a past moment, often explaining a past result.

Positive: had been + V-ing · Negative: hadn't been + V-ing · Question: Had + subject + been + V-ing?

He was tired because he had been running. · They had been waiting for an hour when the bus arrived.

Common markers: forsinceall dayhow long

Focus on duration or the activity itself. Past Perfect often focuses on the completed result.
